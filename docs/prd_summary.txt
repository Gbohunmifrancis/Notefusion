NoteFusion Product Requirements Document (PRD) Hear it. See it. Play it. Version 1.0  •  July 2026 Prepared for: RealDiamond Digitals, Akure, Ondo State, Nigeria Status: Draft — Idea Validation Phase 1. Product Overview NoteFusion is an Android application that listens to live or recorded music through the device microphone and automatically transcribes it into two notation systems: Tonic Solfa (movable-do solfège) and standard Staff Notation. The interaction model is deliberately simple and familiar — a single tap starts listening, similar to Shazam — but where Shazam identifies a song, NoteFusion tells the user exactly what notes are being played, in a form a musician can immediately read, sing, or play back. The product targets a genuine gap: no mainstream transcription app produces Tonic Solfa output, despite solfa being the primary notation system for millions of choir singers, church instrumentalists, and music learners across Nigeria, West Africa, and much of the Commonwealth. Staff-notation transcription apps exist (ScoreCloud, AnthemScore, Piano2Notes), but they are desktop-first, subscription-heavy, and solfa-blind. 1.1 Vision Statement Every musician — from a choir director in Akure to a self-taught pianist anywhere in the world — should be able to capture the music around them and instantly see it written down in the notation they actually read. 1.2 What NoteFusion Is Not It is not a song-recognition app. It does not match audio against a database of recordings (the Shazam/Spotify model). It analyses the audio itself. It is not a DAW or full score editor. Light editing is supported; deep engraving is out of scope for v1. It is not limited to known songs. Original compositions, improvisations, and live playing are first-class inputs — this is precisely what fingerprinting apps cannot do. 2. Problem Statement and Pain Points 2.1 Pain Points Transcription by ear is slow and skill-gated.  Working out a song's notes by ear takes hours and requires trained relative pitch. Most choir directors and instrumentalists transcribe manually, note by note, on paper. Tonic Solfa is underserved by technology.  Solfa is the dominant working notation in Nigerian churches, school music programmes, and choral communities, yet virtually every music-tech tool assumes staff notation only. Users currently convert staff scores to solfa by hand. Existing transcription tools are inaccessible.  AnthemScore is desktop software (~USD 30–100 licence). ScoreCloud is subscription-based and desktop/iOS-centred. Piano2Notes charges per transcription minute. None are built mobile-first for Android, which dominates the target market. Learning materials do not match how people learn.  A pianist who hears a progression in a live service has no practical way to capture it as readable notation before it is forgotten. Rehearsal preparation is a bottleneck.  Choir parts are often taught by rote because producing written solfa parts for each voice takes too long. 2.2 Opportunity Android holds an overwhelming share of the Nigerian smartphone market, and the gospel/choral music ecosystem is one of the most active music communities in the country. A mobile-first, solfa-first transcription tool has no direct competitor. The staff-notation output simultaneously makes the app relevant to the global market of students, hobbyists, and semi-professional musicians. 3. Target Users and Personas Persona Profile Core Need Choir Director / Music Minister Leads a church or school choir; fluent in solfa; teaches parts to singers weekly Capture a melody or part once and get clean solfa sheets to distribute Pianist / Instrumentalist Plays keyboard, sax, or guitar in bands and services; learns largely by ear Turn what they hear (or improvise) into notation they can revisit and refine Music Student / Learner Learning theory, sight-singing, or an instrument; may read solfa, staff, or both See the same music in both notations side by side to bridge the two systems Composer / Songwriter Writes original music, often away from a computer Hum or play an idea and have it captured as notation before it is lost 4. Value Proposition and Differentiation Dual notation output.  The only app producing Tonic Solfa and staff notation from the same tap — the defining differentiator. Mobile-first, one-tap UX.  Shazam-grade simplicity applied to transcription rather than recognition. Works on any music.  Live playing, humming, rehearsals, and original compositions — not just catalogued recordings. Built on efficient open-source ML.  State-of-the-art transcription quality without per-minute cloud fees, keeping the product viable on freemium pricing. Regional insight as moat.  Deep solfa support (key labelling, doh-is-X headers, voice-part conventions) reflects how solfa is actually used in Nigerian choral practice — knowledge global competitors lack. 5. Features and Requirements 5.1 MVP (v1.0) — Must Have ID Feature Description Priority F-01 One-tap capture Large central button starts/stops listening; live waveform feedback; 10s–3min clips; import of existing audio files (MP3/WAV/M4A) P0 F-02 Automatic transcription Audio is converted to note events (pitch, onset, duration) using an ML transcription model; monophonic and moderately polyphonic music supported P0 F-03 Key detection + override Automatic key estimation with a clearly visible, user-editable key (critical: solfa is movable-do, so a wrong key makes all solfa wrong) P0 F-04 Staff notation view Rendered engraved score (treble/bass as appropriate) with correct key signature, time signature, and quantised rhythms P0 F-05 Tonic Solfa view Movable-do solfa (d r m f s l t, with chromatic syllables de/ra etc.), octave marks, bar lines, and a 'Doh is C' style header; toggle or side-by-side with staff view P0 F-06 Playback Play back the transcription as synthesised audio with a moving cursor, so users can verify accuracy by ear P0 F-07 Basic editing Tap a note to correct pitch or duration; transpose; change detected key/tempo; edits update both notation views P0 F-08 Save & library Local library of transcriptions with titles, dates, and detected key P0 F-09 Export & share Export as PDF (staff, solfa, or both), MusicXML, and MIDI; share sheet integration (WhatsApp is the primary sharing channel for the target market) P0 5.2 v1.x — Should Have Tempo tap-in and metronome-guided recording for cleaner rhythm quantisation. Voice-part labelling for choral work (Soprano/Alto/Tenor/Bass headers on exported solfa sheets). Simple chord symbol detection above the staff. Offline transcription mode (on-device model) for low-connectivity environments. 5.3 v2+ — Roadmap Real-time streaming transcription (notes appear as you play). Instrument/vocal source separation so users can transcribe one part out of a full mix. Multi-voice polyphonic separation into SATB parts. Cloud sync, collaboration, and a shared library for choirs. iOS release once the Android product is validated. 5.4 Non-Functional Requirements Category Requirement Accuracy ≥ 90% note-level F1 on clean monophonic recordings; ≥ 75% on clean piano polyphony (measured against reference datasets — see TRD) Latency Transcription result for a 30-second clip returned in ≤ 15 seconds on a mid-range connection (server mode) or ≤ 30 seconds on-device Device support Android 8.0 (API 26)+; must run acceptably on 2–3 GB RAM devices common in the target market Data efficiency Uploaded audio compressed (≤ ~1 MB per 30s clip); app size ≤ 60 MB without offline model, offline model an optional download Privacy Recordings processed transiently; deleted from the server after transcription unless the user opts into cloud backup Reliability Graceful degradation: if transcription confidence is low, tell the user honestly and suggest a cleaner re-record rather than returning garbage 6. Primary User Flow User opens NoteFusion and taps the central Listen button. App records audio with a live waveform; user taps again to stop (or auto-stop at silence). A processing screen shows staged progress (Analysing audio → Detecting notes → Detecting key → Building score). Result screen shows staff notation by default with a Solfa toggle; the detected key ('Doh is F') is shown prominently and is tappable to change. User plays back the result, makes quick corrections, renames, and saves. User exports a solfa PDF and shares it to the choir WhatsApp group. 7. Success Metrics 7.1 Validation-Phase Metrics (first 90 days) ≥ 500 installs from organic/community channels (choirs, music schools, social media) without paid acquisition. ≥ 40% of new users complete a first transcription within one session (activation). ≥ 25% of activated users export or share at least one transcription (the 'it actually worked for me' signal). Qualitative: 15+ structured interviews with choir directors and instrumentalists on output accuracy and solfa correctness. 7.2 Growth-Phase Metrics Week-4 retention ≥ 20%; average ≥ 3 transcriptions per active user per month. Median user-rated transcription quality ≥ 4/5 on in-app feedback prompt. Free-to-paid conversion ≥ 3% once premium tier launches (unlimited length, batch export, offline mode). 8. Monetisation (Proposed) Freemium:  Free tier — clips up to ~60 seconds, watermark-free PDF export of a limited number of scores per month. Premium (monthly/annual, priced for the Nigerian market with regional pricing elsewhere) — longer recordings, unlimited exports, offline mode, choir voice-part sheets. No per-transcription charges.  Competitors charging per minute create anxiety; open-source self-hosted ML keeps marginal cost near zero, making flat pricing viable and a marketing weapon. 9. Risks and Assumptions Risk Impact Mitigation Transcription accuracy disappoints on noisy, real-world audio (phone mics, church reverb, congregational noise) High Set expectations in UX; guide users to record close to the source; show confidence indicators; make correction fast; improve models iteratively Wrong key detection corrupts all solfa output High Always show detected key prominently; one-tap key change instantly re-renders solfa; allow 'Doh is X' manual entry before recording Complex polyphony (full band + vocals) transcribes poorly Medium Position v1 for melody, solo instrument, and piano; source separation is an explicit v2 feature, not an implied v1 promise Server costs grow with usage Medium Efficient model (CPU-friendly), audio compression, queueing; migrate heavy users to on-device inference Users expect Shazam-style song identification Low/Medium Onboarding copy and store listing explicitly frame the product as 'writes down the notes', not 'names the song' Key Assumptions to Validate Solfa-literate musicians will trust and use machine-generated solfa (validate with choir-director interviews and printed sample outputs). One-tap capture on a phone mic yields audio clean enough for usable transcription in typical rehearsal settings. Users will pay a modest subscription for longer recordings and choir features. 11. Idea Validation Plan (Pre-Build and MVP) Week 1–2 — Technical spike: run open-source transcription models (see TRD) on 20 representative recordings — solo keyboard, sung melody, choir rehearsal, phone-mic quality — and manually grade the output. This answers the 'is the core magic good enough?' question before any app code is written. Week 2–3 — Concept test: show 10–15 choir directors/instrumentalists a clickable prototype plus real sample outputs (staff + solfa PDFs generated in the spike). Measure willingness to use and pay. Week 4–10 — Build MVP per this PRD and the TRD. Week 10–14 — Closed beta with 30–50 musicians from local choirs and music communities; instrument the funnel (record → transcribe → edit → export). Decision gate: proceed to public Play Store launch if activation ≥ 40% and median quality rating ≥ 3.5/5 in beta. 12.
