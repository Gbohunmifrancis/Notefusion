This is an intensely well-engineered pipeline. The docstrings reveal a deep, painful battle with the realities of Automatic Music Transcription (AMT)—specifically the fight against `basic_pitch` octave locking, vibrato fragmentation, and `librosa`'s tempogram biases.

The heuristic band-aids here (adaptive amplitude, onset merging, cross-referencing `pyin` with the CNN posterior) are exceptionally clever. However, you have reached the structural limits of what `basic_pitch` and `librosa` can do. To make this engine hyper-accurate—especially for vocal/hummed inputs where tonic solfa is heavily used—you need to swap the acoustic models for ones explicitly trained on modern Music Information Retrieval (MIR) tasks.

Here is the architectural roadmap to drastically improve pitch, time signature, and key/solfa accuracy.

### 1. Pitch & Onset: Replace `pyin` + `basic_pitch` with specialized Vocal Trackers

`basic_pitch` was trained primarily on instrumental MIDI data (guitars, pianos). It struggles with the continuous pitch glides and vibrato of the human voice, which is why it shatters notes and makes whole-octave errors. Running `librosa.pyin` on top is a heavy CPU bottleneck (~0.22x realtime) that only attempts to patch bad data.

**The Solution:**
Switch to a neural pitch tracker designed specifically for monophonic voice and continuous pitch.

* **FCPE (Fast Continuous Pitch Estimator):** Originally built by ByteDance, this model is lightweight, incredibly fast, and practically immune to the octave errors and vibrato wobbles that plague `basic_pitch`.
* **TorchCREPE:** The gold standard for monophonic pitch tracking. It outputs a pitch contour and a highly reliable periodicity (confidence) metric.

**Implementation Strategy:**

1. Run CREPE/FCPE to get a pristine $F_0$ contour without octave jumps.
2. Run a lightweight onset detector (like `madmom.features.onsets.RNNOnsetProcessor`) to find the note starts.
3. Pool the CREPE pitch median *only* within the boundaries of the detected onsets. This eliminates the need for your complex `_merge_onsets` and `_refine_pitches` loops entirely.

### 2. Time Signature: Ditch `librosa` for `madmom` Downbeat Tracking

Line 1221 contains the ultimate surrender: `s.insert(0, meter.TimeSignature("4/4"))`. As you noted, `librosa.beat_track` and acoustic tempograms cannot reliably differentiate 3/4 from 4/4 or 6/8 because they only look for generic periodic energy, not musical downbeats.

**The Solution:**
Use **Madmom** (an advanced Python MIR library) which uses Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) specifically trained to find the *first beat of the bar* (the downbeat).

```python
from madmom.features.downbeats import RNNDownBeatProcessor, DBNDownBeatTrackingProcessor

# 1. Process the audio to find beat probabilities
proc = RNNDownBeatProcessor()(audio_path)

# 2. Track beats and downbeats, allowing 3/4, 4/4, and 6/8 signatures
dbn = DBNDownBeatTrackingProcessor(beats_per_bar=[3, 4, 6], fps=100)
beats_and_downbeats = dbn(proc)

# Output is an array of [time_in_seconds, beat_number_in_bar]
# Example output: [[0.5, 1], [1.0, 2], [1.5, 3], [2.0, 1]] -> clearly 3/4 time!

```

By analyzing the maximum `beat_number_in_bar` returned by the model over the course of the clip, you instantly have the numerator of your time signature, allowing you to dynamically insert `TimeSignature("3/4")` or `TimeSignature("6/8")`.

### 3. Key Detection & Solfa Efficiency: Transition to Functional Harmony

Tonic solfa is entirely dependent on absolute precision of the *Tonic* (Doh). Right now, the pipeline uses an ensemble vote of `music21` Krumhansl-Schmuckler histograms. Histograms are just pitch-class counters; they don't understand functional harmony (e.g., a V -> I cadence). If a user sings a song with a heavy dominant emphasis, the histogram gets confused.

**The Solution:**
Implement a Hidden Markov Model (HMM) chord recognizer to find the *cadence*.

1. Extract the chroma features from the audio.
2. Pass them through an HMM (like `librosa.chord` or Madmom's chord recognition) to get a sequence of chords.
3. **Look at the end of the phrase.** Western music and folk melodies almost exclusively resolve to the Tonic (I) chord at the final note. If the final chord is C Major, and the leading chord is G Major (V -> I), your key is unequivocally C Major.
4. This structural cue is vastly more reliable than a pitch histogram for assigning `Doh`.

### 4. Handling Unaccompanied Pitch Drift (A Cappella Tuning)

When people hum or sing into an app without a backing track, they naturally drift flat or sharp over time. A note that started as a perfectly tuned A4 (440Hz) might drift down to an Ab4 (415Hz) by bar 8.

* Your current quantization snaps pitches globally using a rigid 12-TET grid.
* **The Fix:** Implement a rolling baseline. Track the moving average of the `bend_semitones` over time. If the user drifts flat by 40 cents over 10 seconds, gradually shift the quantization grid down by 40 cents so the later notes aren't abruptly bumped down a semitone and assigned the wrong solfa syllable.

### 5. Backend Optimization

Since this pipeline executes per request:

* **Avoid Subprocesses:** Repeatedly calling `subprocess.run` for FFmpeg introduces high latency. If you must convert formats, use a direct Python binding like `PyAV` to decode the bytes in memory, skipping the disk write entirely.
* **Model Caching:** Ensure whatever ML models you use (whether Basic Pitch, Madmom, or CREPE) are loaded into a global singleton in memory on server boot. Re-initializing PyTorch/TensorFlow graphs on every web request will cripple throughput as concurrency scales.

**High-value upgrades**

  - **PENN** or **Spice** for real-time / low-latency.

  - Estimate local vibrato rate/depth from the contour.  
  - Widen the tolerance only when high vibrato energy is detected.  
  - Use continuous f0 distance + duration overlap + amplitude ratio as a soft cost instead of a hard threshold.

- **Post-quantization pitch correction**  
  After music21 quantization, re-estimate median f0 inside each final note span and snap only when the disagreement is a clear octave or a consistent bias. This catches residual errors that survived the earlier stages.

### 2. Time Signature Detection (currently hardcoded to 4/4)

The code contains a detailed post-mortem explaining why both acoustic and symbolic meter detectors failed on the corpus. That analysis is correct for the current data distribution, but the limitation can be lifted.

**Practical path to real meter detection**

1. **Expand the evaluation corpus** with balanced 3/4, 6/8, 2/4, 5/4, and mixed-meter examples (especially sung material). Without this, any detector will overfit to 4/4.
2. **Two-stage approach**:
   - First estimate tempo and beat phase (already strong).
   - Then score candidate meters `{2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 6/8}` using a combination of:
     - Bar-level onset density / energy periodicity (tempogram ratio at bar rate).
     - Phrase-boundary alignment (longer notes / rests preferentially land on strong beats).
     - Symbolic features after a provisional quantization (coverage of bar starts, long-note contrast).
3. **Use a learned meter model** (e.g., a small Transformer or RNN trained on beat + onset features) rather than hand-crafted rules. Even a lightweight model trained on the expanded corpus will outperform the previous acoustic/symbolic attempts.
4. **Fallback policy**: if confidence is low, keep 4/4 and surface a “meter uncertain” flag so the mobile UI can offer a quick override (mirroring the existing key-override UX).

Until the corpus is expanded, leave the constant 4/4 and invest the engineering time in pitch and key instead.

### 3. Tonic / Key / Solfa Accuracy

**Current strengths**  
Equal-vote ensemble over five music21 profiles + cadence preference + short-clip confidence cap is already better than the single-profile default.

**Further gains**

- **Add a neural key / tonic estimator**  
  Models such as **KeyNet**, **DeepKey**, or a small Transformer trained on pitch-class histograms + rhythmic features often outperform pure profile methods on short / noisy / vibrato-laden clips.  
  Run it in parallel with the ensemble and take a weighted vote (or use the neural model only when the profile margin is low).

- **Melodic contour / phrase-structure features**  
  The current `_cadence_prefers_runner_up` is a good start. Extend it with:
  - Final + initial pitch-class emphasis.
  - Leap vs step statistics.
  - Presence of leading-tone motion.
  These are cheap to compute from the already-quantized stream.

- **Solfa-specific post-processing**  
  After syllable assignment, run a small consistency pass:
  - Prefer spellings that minimize accidental density inside the detected key.
  - Detect and correct systematic octave errors that survive pyin (common on high notes).
  - For movable-do, enforce that the majority of notes land on the expected scale degrees; if they don’t, trigger a key re-evaluation.

- **User override feedback loop**  
  When the mobile user corrects the key, store the (audio features → corrected key) pair. Over time this becomes a valuable fine-tuning set for both the ensemble weights and any neural model.

### 4. Efficiency Improvements

- **Early exit / progressive refinement**  
  - Run a fast Basic Pitch pass first.  
  - If overall confidence is already high and note count is sufficient, skip pyin and the heavier model.  
  - Only refine low-confidence or vibrato-suspect regions.

- **Model quantization & ONNX / TensorRT**  
  Convert Basic Pitch (and any new model) to ONNX or TensorRT for substantially lower latency on CPU/GPU workers.

- **Batch / streaming inference**  
  For longer clips, process in overlapping windows and stitch results. This also improves latency for the user.

- **Caching of intermediate contours**  
  Cache the pyin / CREPE contour keyed by audio hash so repeated jobs (or re-key requests) are nearly free.

- **Parallelize independent stages**  
  Key ensemble profiles, tempo grid search, and audio preview synthesis can run concurrently once the note list is stable.

### 1. Stop asking one transcription model to solve everything

Your current pipeline uses Basic Pitch events and then tries to recover pitch, vibrato, timing, key and notation with post-processing. Your code itself shows that pYIN is being used mainly as a correction layer on Basic Pitch events. 

That is useful, but it has a ceiling.

For mixed music, I would make **two AMT paths** and then fuse their results:

**Path A — Your current Basic Pitch**

Keep it because it is excellent as a lightweight baseline and gives you onset/offset/pitch events.

**Path B — MT3 / YourMT3+**

The important point is that **source separation isn't necessarily the final transcription output**. It is an intermediate representation that makes transcription easier.

```text

```

The **critical conceptual change** is this:

> Don't try to make Basic Pitch increasingly perfect through post-processing.

Instead:

> **Separate → transcribe with multiple models → estimate rhythm/tonality independently → jointly decode the musical structure.**

That is the path most likely to move you from your current **~0.86 note F1 region** toward something substantially more reliable on real-world music rather than merely improving the 38-case heuristic corpus. Your current measurements show that carefully tuned heuristics already helped substantially, but they also show where the approach is hitting structural limits. 

One especially important warning: **do not train or tune your next heuristics only on the existing 38 cases.** Your own post-mortem explicitly found cases where a value fitted to nine vibrato cases produced regressions elsewhere. 

I would therefore make your next version **ensemble + joint-decoding based**, not another accumulation of constants.
